## Cascading integration pipeline
1. Discover accessible repositories, files, skill definitions, notebooks, contracts, archives and historical artifacts.
2. Preserve source path, source revision, content hash, license/status and timestamps where available. Never silently rewrite or merge source material.
3. Classify components as DESIGNED, IMPLEMENTED, TESTED, BENCHMARKED, INDEPENDENTLY_VERIFIED, BLOCKED or HISTORICAL.
4. Map interfaces, dependencies, data formats, permissions, runtime requirements and duplicate candidates without deleting anything.
5. Align shared task-envelope, capability, action-receipt, provenance-receipt and verification-result schemas using versioned adapters.
6. Integrate one bounded, low-risk vertical slice at a time behind explicit permissions and feature flags.
7. Run unit, contract, integration, regression, failure-injection and reproducible benchmark tests.
8. Verify outputs, side effects, hashes and claims; distinguish source-documented claims from observed execution.
9. Append evidence receipts and cross-link them from the control plane.
10. Turn every new opportunity into a child work item with dependencies, value, risk, cost, evidence requirement and next executable action.

## Guardrails
- Source roots remain read-only during discovery and inventory.
- New files use new paths; inventory generation must not overwrite existing artifacts.
- No credentials or private source corpus are transmitted externally by default.
- Deployment, financial transactions, network exposure and irreversible actions require explicit authorization.
- Failed or unrun tests remain unverified.
- A green workflow proves only the checks it actually ran.
- Duplicate candidates are linked for review, never automatically deleted.
- MAI, IIA and MAIA remain distinct until source evidence establishes their relationship.
- Independent branches may run in parallel; dependent stages respect prerequisites.
